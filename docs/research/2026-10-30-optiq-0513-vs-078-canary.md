# OptiQ 0.5.13 against 0.7.8: did the self-update move our numbers? (canary, 2026-10-30)

**Question.** On 2026-10-29 OptiQ updated itself on the host from 0.5.13, the build every published OptiQ number was measured on, to 0.7.8 (STATE Decision 135). Does that jump change what we publish?

**Axis.** Runtime build (OptiQ 0.5.13 vs 0.7.8). Held constant: the artifact (`mlx-community/Qwen3.5-4B-4bit`, stock4bit), the `decode` workload, the harness, the host. **Cannot claim:** anything about other models, other workloads, or any other engine; the 0.5.13 environment is a reconstruction (see Caveats), not the original.

## Result (`ohyesmlx canary`, `--engines optiq`, no `--record`)

| Build | Run | decode tok/s | TTFT p50 s | load before→after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.7.8 (global) | baseline 2026-10-29 | 76.9 | 0.165 | 1.8→2.2 |
| 0.7.8 (global) | sample 2, 2026-10-30 | 79.2 | 0.171 | 3.1→2.5 |
| 0.5.13 (lab copy) | sample 1 | 74.5 | 0.241 | 3.6→2.5 |
| 0.5.13 (lab copy) | sample 2 | 73.7 | 0.239 | 1.9→1.7 |

All four cells PASS the coherence gate. Within a build the two samples agree to about 1% on TTFT; across builds TTFT differs by **about 45%** (0.24 s on 0.5.13, 0.17 s on 0.7.8) in both pairs of samples. Decode is 73.7–74.5 on 0.5.13 against 76.9–79.2 on 0.7.8, a gap of roughly 5% on the means, at the harness noise floor. The canary's verdict for 0.5.13 against the 0.7.8 baseline was "moved +45.8%" and "+44.9%" (TTFT), and "no effect" for the second 0.7.8 sample against its own baseline.

**Reading.** OptiQ's first-token latency on this model dropped by roughly a third from 0.5.13 to 0.7.8 and its decode rate is the same or slightly better. Published OptiQ TTFT figures from the 0.5.x era (grids, prompt-length and cold/warm studies) therefore describe the older build and should be labelled so; decode-rate conclusions are far less exposed. Re-running the OptiQ columns is the decision the verdict asks for and is Jason's call.

## A finding about pinning

The first lab copy failed: every request returned HTTP 404 (`9 of 9 measured requests failed`). `pip install mlx-optiq==0.5.13` does not pin `mlx-lm`; pip resolved `mlx-lm 0.32.2`, whose `mlx_lm.server` answered `POST /v1/chat/completions` 404 (server log: `mlx_lm.server is not recommended for production…`, then 404). Adding `mlx-lm==0.31.3` (the server the harness documents OptiQ as running) made the cell PASS. **A pinned package is not a pinned build.** `engines.install_plan("optiq")` now pins `mlx-lm` to the tested mlx-lm version (test-first), and a full per-venv constraints file is the stronger upgrade if a transitive dependency (`mlx`, `transformers`) drifts.

## Caveats

- Two samples per build, one cell, one workload, 512 decode tokens: a regression detector's resolution, not a study.
- The 0.5.13 environment is a reconstruction: OptiQ 0.5.13, `mlx-lm 0.31.3`, `mlx 0.32.4`, `transformers 5.12.1`, Python 3.14. The original environment's `mlx` and `transformers` versions are not recorded; part of the TTFT difference could come from them rather than from OptiQ.
- Load during runs was 1.7–3.6 (below the canary's 4.0 noisy threshold); Time Machine was in its post-backup thinning phase for the 0.5.13 samples and the second 0.7.8 sample.
- The manifest baseline stays the 0.7.8 one. `lab-paths.json` on this host now points OptiQ at the 0.5.13 lab copy, so the harness runs the tested build; the global 0.7.8 is untouched.
