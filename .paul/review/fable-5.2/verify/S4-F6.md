# S4-F6 — The memory sentences state numbers the fit rule does not use

**Claim, one line:** `_fit` decides and excludes rows on `size_bytes * 2.0` and
`size_bytes * 1.25`, but `_reason` prints "memory to spare" as `budget - size_bytes` (1x) and
`exclusion_reason` prints "Needs about `size_bytes * 2.0` GB at peak" — so a tight row is
offered with positive spare memory while the module's own peak sentence puts it above the
budget, and an excluded row's stated need quotes a line it did not miss.

   (`recommend.py:43`) with the "weights at most 80% of the budget" rationale at `:38-42`.
2. **`_fit` uses only those two.** `size * COMFORTABLE_FACTOR <= budget` is comfortable

5. **`pick` composes the sentence.** It excludes through `exclusion_reason` (`:184-185`) and

| quantity | defined at | value |
|---|---|---|
| comfortable line | `_fit:89`, `COMFORTABLE_FACTOR:36` | `2.0 x size` |
| tight / exclusion line | `_fit:91`, `TIGHT_FACTOR:43` | `1.25 x size` |
| exclusion sentence's "memory at peak" | `:136` | `2.0 x size` |
| offer sentence's "memory to spare" | `:108` | `budget - 1.0 x size` |

|---|---|---|---|
| 16 GiB (9.31 GB) | 1.79 GB | comfortable | "Fits with about 7.5 GB of memory to spare; 1.8 GB download" |

| 8 GiB (3.58 GB) | 1.82 GB | tight | "Tight fit, about 1.8 GB of memory to spare; 1.8 GB download" |

Consequences, all confirmed by the tests:

- **Comfortable rows overstate spare by their own size.** The 2B oQ4 on 16 GiB is comfortable
  because `2 x 1.79 = 3.59 GB` fits the 9.31 GB budget; the headroom over that line is
  5.7 GB, and the sentence says 7.5.
- **Tight rows can claim spare while the module's own peak sentence is above the budget.**
  The 4.78 GB row on 16 GiB prints 4.5 GB to spare, while the same module says a 4.78 GB row
  needs 9.6 GB at peak against a 9.3 GB budget.
- **Excluded rows are attributed to the wrong line.** The 3.06 GB row on 8 GiB is left out
  because `1.25 x 3.06 = 3.83 GB > 3.58 GB`; the sentence says it needs 6.1 GB — 60% above
  the threshold that actually excluded it.

## The D5 wording check

The finding says confidence rises if D5's wording decision does not bless the 1x
subtraction. It does not. PLAN D5 (`PLAN.md:18`) says only that "Fits" is computed from the
user's RAM/disk and labelled estimated. The interface pins the fit at twice/1.25x
`size_bytes` against `model_budget_bytes` (`docs/interfaces/v5-onboarding.md:76-79`) and calls
the exclusion text "a fixed-template reason naming the memory or disk shortfall"
(`:93-96`) — no multiplier for either sentence. PLAN T3.3's example is "fits with 6 GB
spare" (`PLAN.md:100`), an example, not an arithmetic definition. Nothing in the pinned
wording chooses `budget - size`.

## Severity check

Agree with Medium. The fit decisions, the exclusion boundary and the ranking are internally
consistent with their documented thresholds, and no measurement or persisted record is
involved — this is the copy that reads those decisions out. What is wrong is the number the
screen exists to show: the spare figure differs from the headroom over the deciding line by
the row's own size on every offered row (up to 4.8 GB here), a tight row can print positive
spare while the module's own peak sentence is above the budget, and an excluded row's stated
need is 60% above the line that excluded it. It is user-facing on the recommendations screen
(`ModelsView.swift:123`, `:39`). Not High: no gate, ranking or record changes, and the tight
line still keeps every offered row inside its documented loadability. Not Low: the memory
headroom is the headline figure of this screen and it is derived from a multiplier the
module uses nowhere else.
