# S3-F9 — Results drops the run's own contention flag and tie list, then calls one engine "fastest" and "Best on your Mac"

**Claim (one line):** the finished summary carries `load.stage_a/b.label` (the harness's own
"this Mac was busy") and `stage_a.tie` (the engines inside the noise floor), the app decodes
both and renders neither, so a stage measured under contention is shown as a clean result and
one engine inside the tie band is badged "fastest in stage" under "Best on your Mac".

Both halves reproduce. Neither field is read anywhere in the view target.

## What I did not verify

- **The actual rendered pixels.** No view was instantiated: the executable target is not a
  test dependency, and instantiating a SwiftUI `Form` in an XCTest needs a host app. The scan
  proves *no reader exists*; it does not prove what a fixed screen looks like.
- **Which condition the harness would hit in practice.** `LOAD_NOISY = 4.0` on a 1-minute load
  average is reachable (`AGENTS.md` records a real 2026-09-16 contention event at 68→40→75
  tok/s), but I ran no measurement, per the order.
- **Whether `tie` occurs on real runs.** The stub ships `tie: []`; I synthesised the two-name
  tie from `TIE = 0.05` and `_winner`'s logic (`quickbench.py:390-394`), which is code-read,
  not observed. A 5% band between two local engines on the same format is plausible but
  unmeasured here.
- **"Best on your Mac" as copy.** `docs/design/phase5/comps.html:460` pins that heading, so the
  wording is Jason's product call. The defect is the missing qualifier, not the heading.

## Severity

The finding says Medium, confidence 80. I agree with Medium, and the missing qualifiers are a
real instance of the project's "a published table carries its caveat" rule
(`docs/interfaces/v5-quickbench.md:144` — "the summary carries each stage's caveat, and every
cell carries the label") failing at the final step.

It is not High: no number is fabricated, the correct figures are on screen, the summary's
payload survives intact in `summaryData` and in the exported JSON (Export re-serialises every
field, AC-2, so the caveat is present in the artifact even though it is absent from the
screen), and the run's own record is on disk. It is not Low: the two statements the screen
makes — "quick: measured on this Mac" beside a contended number, and "fastest in stage" /
"Best on your Mac" over a tie — are exactly the claims the noisier number cannot support, and
they are read as the run's verdict. The user has no way to learn from the app that the Mac was
busy or that two engines were indistinguishable; the label exists only in the JSON they would
have to open themselves.
